Renewable energy has become the backbone of sustainable urban planning. Modern cities rely heavily on electricity, and clean sources such as solar, wind, and hydro power allow them to grow without the pollution once caused by coal plants. Installing rooftop solar panels helps households cut their bills while feeding excess power back into the grid. Meanwhile, wind farms placed offshore or on surrounding plains can supply steady energy without occupying valuable city land. Hydroelectric stations provide baseload support, stabilizing the electrical system during peak demand. As cities expand their public transit systems, renewable energy keeps trains and buses moving with lower emissions. This shift not only improves local air quality but also creates new jobs for engineers, technicians, and maintenance crews. Ultimately, investing in renewable energy strengthens urban resilience, making cities healthier, more efficient, and better prepared for future challenges.
